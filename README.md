# Lost Audio VST3 Suite

A free set of lo-fi/character plugins focused on radio, media, and degraded playback textures.

## Install (Windows)

1. Each plugin is a `.vst3` bundle. On Windows, this is normal and often appears as a folder.
2. Copy the `.vst3` bundles into your VST3 folder:
   - `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\` (system-wide, may require admin)
   - or your DAW's custom VST3 folder
3. Restart your DAW and run a plugin rescan.

## Ableton / Audacity Notes

- Ableton Live 11/12: rescan plugins in Preferences, then load from VST3.
- Audacity: scan for plugins, then enable each effect in Plugin Manager.

## Plugins

- **Transmission Engine**: portable radio/comms tone with tuning behavior and gritty voice focus.
- **Tape Engine**: cassette-style wear, compression, instability, and soft degradation.
- **Television Engine**: old TV speaker vibe, bandwidth limits, and broadcast-style coloration.
- **CD Engine**: digital-era harshness, edge, and controlled artifacts.
- **Comms Engine**: utility/radio communication voicing and constrained band shaping.
- **Conference Engine**: speakerphone/meeting-room tone with distance and intelligibility shaping.
- **Camcorder Engine**: consumer camera audio character with narrow, noisy, vintage response.
- **Cartridge Engine**: retro game/media coloration with dirt, edge, and limited fidelity.
- **Open Mic Night**: stage-style feedback/room coloration for live-ish performance texture.

## Presets vs. Tuning

Presets are starting points, not finished masters.

- Use presets to land in the right neighborhood quickly.
- Then adjust parameters by ear for your source, arrangement, and genre.
- Small moves usually win: 5-15% changes can radically affect intelligibility and vibe.
- Automate key controls (drive, tone, mix, tuning amount, etc.) for movement through sections.

If a preset sounds too extreme, pull back damage/noise first, then refine filtering and mix.
